Digital Assessment and Feedback Tools

Checking learning and supporting improvement

Week Overview

Digital assessment uses technology to gather evidence of student learning. It can support quick checks, assignments, portfolios, peer assessment, and feedback. Assessment should match learning outcomes and be fair, clear, and accessible.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, students will be able to:

  • Differentiate formative and summative assessment.
  • Identify digital tools for quizzes, assignments, and portfolios.
  • Explain the role of timely feedback in learning.
  • Recognise issues of fairness, validity, privacy, and accessibility.

Topics and Study Notes

Formative assessment

Ongoing checks such as polls, short quizzes, exit tickets, and discussion prompts help teachers adjust instruction.

Summative assessment

End of unit tests, projects, presentations, and final portfolios measure achievement after a period of learning.

Digital assessment tools

Online forms, LMS quizzes, interactive question platforms, e portfolios, and rubric tools can support assessment tasks.

Feedback and analytics

Specific feedback explains what was done well and what to improve. Learning analytics can show patterns, but teachers should interpret data carefully.

Fair and responsible assessment

Use clear criteria, accessible formats, suitable time limits, privacy protection, and more than one kind of evidence when appropriate.

Related Study Material

  1. Compare formative and summative assessment examples.
  2. Create five questions for a short online quiz.
  3. Review a rubric and identify how it makes expectations clear.

Complete Study Notes

These notes explain the main topics of this week in simple academic English. Read each section and review the examples.

Meaning of emerging technologies

Emerging technologies are newer or rapidly developing tools that may change how people teach, learn, communicate, and practise skills. In education, examples include artificial intelligence, augmented reality, virtual reality, simulations, adaptive learning systems, and robotics. Their value depends on whether they support a real learning need.

Example: Teachers should evaluate educational benefit, cost, safety, accessibility, and evidence before adopting a new tool.

Artificial intelligence in education

Artificial intelligence, or AI, refers to computer systems that perform tasks that usually require human abilities such as recognising patterns, generating text, translating language, or responding to questions. AI tools can support tutoring, language practice, idea generation, feedback, and adapting content to learner needs.

Example: AI can make mistakes, produce biased information, or give answers without reliable sources. Students and teachers should check important information and use AI honestly.

Augmented reality and virtual reality

Augmented reality, or AR, adds digital objects or information to a view of the real world. It is often used through a phone or tablet. Virtual reality, or VR, creates a computer generated environment that a learner can explore as if inside a different place. A headset is commonly used.

Example: An AR app may show labels for parts of a plant through a phone camera. A VR experience may let students explore a virtual ancient city.

Simulations and virtual laboratories

A simulation is a digital model of a real or imagined process. Learners can change inputs, observe results, and practise decisions in a controlled environment. Virtual laboratories allow students to carry out simulated experiments when physical equipment, time, or safety may be a concern.

Example: Simulations support practice, but they may not reproduce every detail of a real situation. Where possible, combine them with practical activities.

Benefits, risks, and responsible use

Emerging technologies can make abstract ideas easier to explore, offer personalised practice, and provide safe opportunities to practise. Limitations may include high cost, technical problems, unequal access, privacy concerns, inaccurate AI output, and excessive screen time. Responsible use includes checking accuracy, avoiding bias, respecting copyright, and following academic honesty rules.

Example: Teachers should explain how tools may be used and remain responsible for guidance and human judgement.

Complete Study Notes

These notes explain the main topics of this week in simple academic English. Read each section and review the examples.

Digital learning resources

Digital learning resources are electronic materials used to support teaching and learning. Examples include e books, presentations, educational websites, podcasts, videos, animations, simulations, infographics, digital worksheets, and online libraries. They may be used in class, at home, or in online courses.

Example: A teacher can use an animation to show how a plant grows or an e book to provide reading material on a phone.

Multimedia learning

Multimedia learning uses more than one form of communication, such as words, pictures, sound, video, and animation. When selected carefully, these forms can help learners understand information that may be difficult to explain through text alone. Each media element should support the learning objective.

Example: A labelled animation with a short explanation may help students understand the movement of blood through the heart.

Text, images, audio, and video

Text is useful for definitions, instructions, and detailed explanations. Images and diagrams show shapes, relationships, and processes. Audio can support listening and language practice. Video can show movement, demonstrations, and real life examples. Teachers should choose the format that best explains the content.

Example: Too many pictures, sounds, or effects can distract learners. Clear and relevant media are more useful than decoration.

Design principles and cognitive load

Learning materials should have a clear structure, readable fonts, suitable contrast, and a consistent layout. Related information should be placed together. Long explanations can be divided into smaller sections. Cognitive load is the mental effort needed to process information. Learners have limited working memory, so materials should not present too much unfamiliar information at once.

Example: A presentation slide should focus on one main idea rather than displaying a full page of small text.

Accessibility, quality, and copyright

Accessible resources can be used by learners with different abilities and needs. Videos should include captions, images should have meaningful alternative text, and text should be readable. A good resource should be accurate, relevant, current, and suitable for learners. Teachers should check the author, organisation, date, evidence, and purpose. Copyright rules must be respected and sources should be credited.

Example: Providing a written transcript with an audio lesson gives learners another way to access the same information.

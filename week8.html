Week Overview

Successful technology integration connects tools with curriculum, teaching methods, and learner needs. Teachers also need to consider digital citizenship, privacy, copyright, accessibility, equity, and the changing role of technology in education.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, students will be able to:

  • Explain a framework for purposeful technology integration.
  • Identify ethical and legal issues in digital learning.
  • Describe ways to improve accessibility and reduce the digital divide.
  • Develop a balanced plan for technology use in a lesson.

Topics and Study Notes

Purposeful integration

Technology should support the learning outcome, teaching method, and assessment. A tool is useful when it improves access, understanding, practice, or feedback.

Digital citizenship

Responsible online behaviour includes respectful communication, checking information, protecting accounts, and understanding one's digital footprint.

Privacy and copyright

Collect only necessary learner data, use secure platforms, respect copyright, and credit sources. Follow institutional rules for student information.

Accessibility and equity

Provide captions, readable formats, alternative ways to participate, and offline options where possible. Consider device and internet access.

Future trends and teacher role

AI supported learning, open resources, learning analytics, and immersive media may grow. Teachers remain responsible for guidance, judgement, relationships, and inclusion.

Related Study Material

  1. Review a technology integration framework such as TPACK or SAMR.
  2. Prepare a checklist for privacy, copyright, and accessibility.
  3. Write a short reflection on the future role of teachers in technology supported learning.

Suggested video resource: Watch related educational videos on YouTube

Class Activity

Create a complete technology integration plan for one lesson. Include the learning outcome, tool, teaching strategy, assessment, accessibility support, and privacy consideration.

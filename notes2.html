These notes explain the main topics of this week in simple academic English. Read each section and review the examples.

Understanding learning theories

Learning theories explain how people gain knowledge, develop skills, remember information, and change their understanding. Teachers use these theories to plan activities and select suitable teaching methods. Educational technology becomes more effective when it is matched with the way learners are expected to learn.

Example: A digital quiz may support practice and feedback, while a collaborative document may support discussion and shared learning.

Behaviourism

Behaviourism focuses on observable behaviour and the way practice, reinforcement, and feedback influence learning. Learners repeat an activity and receive a response that helps strengthen correct performance. Digital flashcards, spelling drills, practice quizzes, and skill exercises can support behaviourist learning.

Example: A learner answers vocabulary questions and receives immediate feedback. Repeated practice helps the learner remember the correct words.

Cognitivism

Cognitivism explains learning through mental processes such as attention, memory, understanding, and problem solving. Learners receive information, organise it, connect it with previous knowledge, and store it in memory. Teachers can support this process by dividing complex content into smaller parts and using clear headings, concept maps, diagrams, and examples.

Example: A labelled diagram of the human heart can help students organise information about its parts and functions.

Constructivism and social learning

Constructivism suggests that learners actively build knowledge by connecting new information with what they already know. They learn through exploration, questions, problem solving, projects, and reflection. Social learning highlights learning through observing others, modelling, discussion, and interaction. Videos, live demonstrations, peer feedback, and group tasks can support these approaches.

Example: Students may watch a teacher demonstrate a spreadsheet task, practise it, and help one another. They can also use a simulation to explore a science concept.

Connectivism and choosing a suitable tool

Connectivism focuses on learning through connections with people, digital resources, and networks. Learners need to find useful information, judge its reliability, and keep their knowledge updated. Teachers should begin with the learning outcome and teaching approach, then choose a tool that supports the task. The same tool can support different theories depending on how it is used.

Example: A student researching climate change may compare a digital library, a scientific website, and an online discussion. The student should check sources instead of accepting every result.

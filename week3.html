Week Overview

Instructional design is a systematic process for planning effective learning experiences. The ADDIE model provides five connected stages: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, students will be able to:

  • Define instructional design and its purpose.
  • Describe the five stages of the ADDIE model.
  • Identify learner needs and measurable learning outcomes.
  • Explain how evaluation improves an instructional plan.

Topics and Study Notes

Analysis

Identify learners, prior knowledge, learning needs, context, available resources, and barriers.

Design

Write measurable objectives, organise content, select teaching strategies, plan activities, and decide how learning will be assessed.

Development

Create or collect lesson materials such as slides, videos, worksheets, quizzes, and digital activities.

Implementation

Deliver the planned instruction, guide learners, provide support, and manage the learning environment.

Evaluation

Use formative and summative evidence to judge effectiveness and make improvements.

Related Study Material

  1. Study the five ADDIE stages and their purpose.
  2. Review examples of measurable learning outcomes using action verbs.
  3. Prepare a one page ADDIE plan for a short lesson.

Suggested video resource: Watch related educational videos on YouTube

Class Activity

Work through ADDIE for a 30 minute lesson. Write one practical action for each stage and share how evaluation may change the next lesson.

Complete Study Notes

These notes explain the main topics of this week in simple academic English. Read each section and review the examples.

Meaning of digital assessment

Digital assessment is the use of electronic tools to check, record, and understand student learning. It may include online quizzes, polls, assignments, e portfolios, interactive tasks, and digital rubrics. Digital tools can make it easier to collect responses and provide feedback, but assessment must still measure the intended learning outcomes.

Example: A teacher may use a short online quiz to check whether students understood the main points of a lesson.

Formative assessment

Formative assessment takes place during the learning process. It helps teachers and students understand what has been learned and what needs more practice. Examples include short quizzes, polls, questioning, exit tickets, drafts, and discussion prompts. Teachers use the results to adjust teaching.

Example: If many students select the same incorrect answer, the teacher can explain that concept again with a new example.

Summative assessment

Summative assessment measures learning at the end of a unit, course, or learning period. Examples include final tests, projects, presentations, and completed portfolios. It is often used to record achievement or assign grades.

Example: A final online test can assess the knowledge and skills students developed during a course.

Digital tools, feedback, and analytics

Teachers can use online forms, LMS quizzes, interactive question platforms, e portfolios, digital rubrics, and assignment tools. Feedback tells learners what they did well, where they made mistakes, and how they can improve. Learning analytics means collecting and examining information about learning activities, such as quiz scores and assignment completion. Data should be interpreted carefully.

Example: Instead of writing only 'Good work', a teacher can explain what was done well and what needs to be improved.

Fairness, validity, and privacy

A fair assessment gives learners an appropriate chance to show what they know. Validity means the assessment measures what it is intended to measure. Reliability means results are reasonably consistent. Clear instructions, accessible formats, and transparent marking criteria improve quality. Teachers should protect names, grades, and submitted work and share them only with authorised people.

Example: If the objective is to assess speaking, a multiple choice test alone may not be suitable.

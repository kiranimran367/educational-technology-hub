Complete Study Notes

These notes explain the main topics of this week in simple academic English. Read each section and review the examples.

Meaning and purpose of instructional design

Instructional design is a planned process for creating effective lessons, courses, and learning materials. It begins by identifying what learners need to know or do. It then organises objectives, content, teaching methods, activities, resources, and assessment. Good instructional design makes learning purposeful, clear, and suitable for learners.

Example: When planning a lesson on fractions, a teacher checks what students already know, sets an objective, prepares examples, chooses an activity, and decides how to check understanding.

The ADDIE model

ADDIE is a common instructional design model. Its five stages are Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation. The stages are connected and may be revisited when improvements are needed. ADDIE helps teachers plan systematically instead of preparing activities without a clear sequence.

Example: The model can be used for a single classroom lesson, an online module, a training session, or a complete course.

Analysis and Design

In Analysis, the teacher studies learners' age, prior knowledge, abilities, interests, language, needs, and possible difficulties. The teacher also considers the topic, time, classroom conditions, devices, internet access, and resources. In Design, the teacher writes measurable objectives, organises topics, chooses teaching strategies, plans activities, selects resources, and decides how learning will be assessed.

Example: Before preparing an online lesson, a teacher checks whether students have smartphones and reliable internet. The teacher then writes a clear objective and plans suitable activities.

Development and Implementation

In Development, the planned materials are created or collected. These may include slides, worksheets, videos, audio recordings, reading materials, diagrams, quizzes, and online activities. Materials should match the objectives and be checked for accuracy and accessibility. Implementation is the stage in which the lesson is delivered. The teacher shares resources, explains instructions, guides activities, manages time, and supports learners.

Example: A teacher may prepare a presentation and quiz, check the meeting link, upload materials, and explain how students should submit their work.

Evaluation and learning objectives

Evaluation checks whether instruction worked and whether learners achieved the objectives. Formative evaluation happens during learning through questions and feedback. Summative evaluation happens at the end through a test, project, or final assignment. A learning objective describes what a learner should be able to do. Strong objectives use action verbs such as identify, explain, compare, demonstrate, design, or evaluate.

Example: If students perform poorly on a quiz, the teacher may review the explanation, add examples, or change the activity. This can lead to improvements in the next lesson.

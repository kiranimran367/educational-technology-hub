Complete Study Notes

These notes explain the main topics of this week in simple academic English. Read each section and review the examples.

Purposeful technology integration

Technology integration means including appropriate tools and resources in teaching and learning in a planned way. Purposeful integration connects technology with the curriculum, learning objectives, teaching methods, assessment, and learner needs. A tool should improve understanding, access, practice, communication, or feedback.

Example: Before using an app, a teacher should be able to explain what students will learn through it and why it is suitable.

TPACK framework

TPACK stands for Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge. It describes the knowledge teachers need to combine subject content, teaching methods, and technology. Content knowledge is what is taught. Pedagogical knowledge is how it is taught. Technological knowledge is how tools work and can be used. Effective integration brings these areas together.

Example: For a science lesson, a teacher needs subject knowledge, a suitable teaching approach, and the ability to use a simulation that explains the concept.

SAMR model

SAMR is a framework for thinking about how technology changes a learning task. Substitution means technology replaces a traditional tool with little change. Augmentation adds a functional improvement. Modification allows significant redesign of the task. Redefinition makes a new task possible that was difficult or impossible before.

Example: Typing an essay instead of handwriting may be substitution. Writing together online with comments from another class may move toward modification.

Digital citizenship, privacy, and copyright

Digital citizenship means using technology in a safe, respectful, responsible, and thoughtful way. It includes respectful communication, protecting accounts, checking information, and understanding digital footprints. Privacy means keeping personal information safe. Copyright protects original work such as books, images, videos, and presentations. Users should follow permission and licence rules and acknowledge sources.

Example: A class list or student grades should not be posted publicly. Students should cite sources and avoid copying another person's work.

Accessibility, equity, and future teacher role

Accessibility means designing learning so people with different abilities can use materials. This may include captions, readable text, alternative text, keyboard access, and clear layouts. The digital divide is the gap between people who have reliable access to devices, internet, and digital skills and those who do not. Future tools may include AI, open resources, learning analytics, and immersive media. Teachers will continue to guide learning, check information, support inclusion, and make professional decisions.

Example: Teachers can offer downloadable or printed materials and more than one way to participate. Technology can support a teacher, but it cannot replace professional judgement and care.
